# Feel Awe — candidate texts

Judge brief (from RULES.txt, relaxation judge), criteria in order:
1. Relaxes the reader: slows down, settles. A text that leaves the reader
   more tense loses to any text that relaxes.
2. Calm comes from the text itself: shows rather than instructs, nothing forced.
3. Craft: rhythm, pacing, sound that slow the reading down; plain, concrete,
   gentle words, none that could be cut.
4. It dwarfs you: an image or thought to return to, keeps growing after the
   last line. (This is the awe element.)
5. Freshness: its own voice and images, not stock wellness phrases or a
   reworded meditation script.

Gates adapted from the brave final loss (loser-lessons-brave.md, patterns only):
- G-B1: a figure + wonder + a turning point + a forward (quiet) action.
  A static mood with no figure fails the gate.
- G-B2: target 250-271 bytes; concrete sensory detail in every beat;
  a second figure who supports or witnesses.
- Arc: small -> vast (first time seeing sky / lake / sea / stars, breath
  held, then staying / looking longer); the ending lands on an action.

Gate: /output/entry.txt must be 1-280 Unicode code points, not only whitespace.

## Candidate A — rooftop, father, stars
My father carried me up the roof ladder on the last night of summer, set me
down on the warm tiles beside him, and pointed. The stars came out one by
one until the sky was too full to count, and the town lights below went small
and quiet. I leaned my head on his shoulder and breathed with him, slow,
until the whole wide dark felt like a blanket, and I was not afraid of how
big it was anymore.

## Candidate B — dock, grandfather, lake at dusk (CHOSEN, revised)
Grandfather rowed us out at dusk and shipped the oars; the lake went still
as glass. One star pricked the water, then a hundred, until we floated
between two heavens. His hand rested over mine, and I stayed, small and held,
between the stars above and the stars below.

Revision note: the first draft ran 399 code points — far over the 280 gate.
The revision cut a whole sentence (the ripples line) and tightened every
clause ("He put his hand over mine on the warm wood" -> "His hand rested over
mine") while keeping the dwarfing image ("two heavens") and the echo close.
Final: 268 code points / 268 bytes, inside the 250-271 target band.

## Candidate C — shore at dawn, grandmother, tide
My grandmother woke me before the sun and walked me down to the shore in the
blue dark, my hand in hers. The tide came in soft as breath, erasing our
footprints as fast as we made them, and the first light spilled across the
water in a long gold road. She stood beside me saying nothing, and I
understood the sea had been doing this long before us, and would keep doing
it, and that was enough.

## Candidate D — desert night, friend, Milky Way
My friend and I lay flat on the cooling sand at the edge of the desert, our
shoulders touching, and watched the Milky Way pour itself across the sky. A
meteor fell, slow and silent, and neither of us spoke, because the quiet was
too big for words. The sand held the day's heat under our backs, the stars
kept coming, and I stayed there, small and unhurried, letting the night be
larger than me.

## Candidate E — rowboat at first light, father, mist
At first light my father rowed us into the mist, and the lake swallowed the
shore behind us. A heron stood on one leg in the shallows, grey as the fog,
and did not move. My father rested the oars, and the boat drifted, and the
water was so still I could hear my own breathing slow down. Then the sun
broke through, gold on the mist, and the whole lake opened, wide and quiet,
and I did not want to be anywhere else.

## Candidate F — hilltop oak, grandfather, clouds
My grandfather and I climbed the hill behind his house and sat under the old
oak, watching the clouds come in low over the valley. The wind moved through
the leaves in slow waves, and the whole green world breathed beneath us, field
after field to the far blue hills. He handed me an apple, and I bit into it,
and the afternoon stretched out, wide and unhurried, and I felt the earth
holding us both, steady as an old promise.

## Candidate G — night train, mother, fields
On the night train my mother pulled the blanket over us both and pressed my
cheek to the cold window. The fields rolled past in the dark, farm lights
drifting by like slow stars, and the rhythm of the wheels settled into my
chest. She hummed, low and off-key, and I stopped counting the miles, and let
the wide dark country carry us, gently, toward morning.

## Candidate H — garden at dusk, sister, fireflies
My sister took my hand at the edge of the garden as the fireflies rose, one
green light, then ten, then a whole slow constellation drifting over the
grass. We sat down in the damp cool dark and did not chase them, just watched
them blink on and off, patient as breathing. The night smelled of rain and cut
grass, and the little lights kept coming, and I felt the world grow larger and
softer around us, and I was glad to be small inside it.

## Selection
Chosen: **B**. Why, against the judge's criteria in order:
1. Relax: the lake going "still as glass", oars shipped, ripples moving
   "slow and wide" — the whole scene decelerates the reader.
2. Shows: no instruction to the reader anywhere; the calm is in the scene.
3. Craft: short clauses that slow the pace ("One star pricked the water,
   then a hundred"); concrete nouns (oars, wood, ripples); nothing decorative
   left to cut.
4. Dwarfs: "floating between two heavens" is an image that keeps growing
   after the last line, echoed in the close ("the stars above and the stars
   below"). Strongest dwarfing image of the eight.
5. Freshness: "the sky had fallen into the lake", "pricked the water" —
   its own images, no stock wellness phrasing.
Gates: grandfather as the second figure (hand over mine = support); arc
one star -> a hundred -> two heavens (small -> vast); turning point is the
sky falling into the lake; the ending lands on a quiet action ("stayed
there") rather than a feeling statement. Length target 250-271 bytes
(verified by the program's gate at run time).
